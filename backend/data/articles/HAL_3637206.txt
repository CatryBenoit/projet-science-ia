Abstract 27 
Previous studies have established the role of Na
+
/H
+
 exchanger isoform-1 (NHE1) and 28 
cathepsin B (Cat B) in the development of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy (CH). Both NHE1 and 29 
Cat B are activated under acidic conditions suggesting that their activities might be 30 
interrelated. The inhibition of NHE1 has been demonstrated to reduce cardiac hypertrophy 31 
but the mechanism that contributes to the anti-hypertrophic effect of NHE1 inhibition still 32 
remains unclear. H9c2 cardiomyoblasts were stimulated with Angiotensin (Ang) II in the 33 
presence and absence of N-[2-methyl-4,5-bis(methylsulphonyl)-benzoyl]-guanidine, 34 
hydrochloride (EMD, EMD 87580), an NHE1 inhibitor or CA-074Me, a Cat B inhibitor, and 35 
various cardiac hypertrophic parameters, namely cell surface area, protein content and atrial 36 
natriuretic peptide (ANP) mRNA were analyzed. EMD significantly suppressed markers of 37 
cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and inhibited Ang II stimulated Cat B protein and gene 38 
expression. Cat B is located within the acidic environment of lysosomes. Cat B proteases are 39 
released into the cytoplasm upon disintegration of the lysosomes. EMD or CA-074Me 40 
prevented the dispersal of the lysosomes induced by Ang II and reduced the ratio of LC3-II to 41 
LC3-I, a marker of autophagy. Moreover, Cat B protein expression and MMP-9 activity in 42 
the extracellular space were significantly attenuated in the presence of EMD or CA-074Me. 43 
Our study demonstrates a novel mechanism for attenuation of the hypertrophic phenotype by 44 
NHE1 inhibition that is mediated by a regression in Cat B. The inhibition of Cat B via EMD 45 
or CA-074Me attenuates the autosomal-lysosomal pathway and MMP-9 activation. 46 
Key words: cardiomyocytes, hypertrophy, autophagy, cathepsins, angiotensin, matrix 47 
metalloproteinases.  48

1. Introduction 49 
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) continue to be the major reason for death globally, 50 
regardless of the progress in treatment (WHO, 2013). The World Health Organization 51 
forecasts that by 2030, heart failure will be a leading cause of death (Mathers CD, 2006; 52 
WHO, 2011). Various CVDs including cardiac hypertrophy (CH), hypertension and 53 
myocardial infarctions progress to heart failure if left untreated (de Couto et al., 2010; 54 
Dupree, 2010). Pathological CH, induced by neurohormonal stimulation, hypertension or 55 
myocardial infarctions (MI), is characterized by the increase in size of cardiomyocytes and 56 
the remodeling of the extracellular matrix (ECM) (Kang and Izumo, 2003; Kehat and 57 
Molkentin, 2010; Mlih et al., 2015; Watkins et al., 2011). The abnormal stimulation of 58 
various ECM proteases including cathepsins and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) have 59 
been proposed to contribute to the progression of cardiac remodeling (Abdulrahman et al., 60 
2018; Cheng et al., 2012; Dhalla et al., 2009; Rodriguez et al., 2010; Wilson EM, 2001). 61 
 62 
The cysteine proteases, cathepsins, have been demonstrated to contribute to cardiac 63 
remodeling (Brömme and Wilson, 2011; Liu et al., 2013; Müller and Dhalla, 2012; Wu et al., 64 
2015). Previous studies have demonstrated that stimulation of H9c2 cardiomyoblasts with 65 
Ang II, a hypertrophic stimulator (Wu et al., 2015). Cat B protein expression and the 66 
inhibition of Cat B resulted in attenuation of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy (Wu et al., 2015). 67 
Similarly, in vivo, the knockout of Cat B attenuated pressure overload-induced CH, fibrosis, 68 
and apoptosis (Wu et al., 2015). Cat B mRNA and protein expression were demonstrated to 69 
be increased in human dilated cardiomyopathy (Ge et al., 2006). Clearly, Cat B has a crucial 70 
function in hypertrophy.  71

Acidification of the peri- and extracellular spaces in  settings of altered cell proliferation has 73 
been shown to activate Cat B (Bourguignon et al., 2004; Greco et al., 2014; Rozhin et al., 74 
1994).  In various forms of carcinomas, this type of pericellular acidification is caused by the 75 
stimulation of the Na
+
/H
+
 exchanger isoform 1 (NHE1), a regulator of intracellular pH 76 
(Bourguignon et al., 2004; Malo and Fliegel, 2006). NHE1 is a ubiquitously expressed 77 
housekeeping glycoprotein, which exchanges one intracellular H
+
 for one extracellular Na
+ 
78 
and is the only cardiac specific plasma membrane isoform of the NHE family (Mohamed and 79 
Mraiche, 2015). Increased activation of NHE1 is a key factor for the development of various 80 
CVDs including CH (Fliegel, 2009; Liu et al., 2013; Wu et al., 2015; Xue et al., 2010). 81 
Inhibition of NHE1, both in vivo and in vitro, has been demonstrated to regress hypertrophy 82 
(Javadov et al., 2008; Javadov et al., 2009; Marano et al., 2004; Mohamed and Mraiche, 83 
2015). The cellular mechanism by which NHE1 inhibition suppresses the hypertrophic 84 
response is not known. However, stimulation of NHE1 is known to promote Cat B activity in 85 
breast cancer cells (Bourguignon et al., 2004) suggesting that NHE1 and Cat B activities 86 
might be related. Whether the anti-hypertrophic effects of NHE1 inhibitors act downstream 87 
through Cat B in cardiomyocytes is not known. 88 
 89 
It has also been suggested that in a malignant cell line, an increase in the pericellular acidic 90 
pH results in the relocation of the lysosomes. The acidic pericellular pH also enhances the 91 
secretion of Cat B into the extracellular space (Rozhin et al., 1994). Furthermore, the release 92 
of Cat B into the ECM induces the activation of MMP-9 (Giusti et al., 2008). In the 93 
myocardium, MMP-9 contributes to ECM remodeling and failure (Singh et al., 2004; 94 
Stempien-Otero et al., 2006). Interestingly, MMP-9 activity has also been demonstrated to be 95 
enhanced in CCL39 lung fibroblasts upon the activation of NHE1 with phenylephrine (Taves 96 
et al., 2008). Recently, the interaction of Cat B and MMP-9 with NHE1 has been shown to 97 
Journal Pre-proof
5 
promote ECM degradation in breast cancer (Greco et al., 2014). These studies suggest a 98 
putative pathway by which NHE1 inhibition reduces Cat B and in turn prevents MMP-9 99 
activation and protects against hypertrophy.  100

3. Results 208

3.1.Ang II stimulates a concentration dependent increase in Cat B protein 210 
expression in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts  211 
H9c2 cardiomyoblasts were treated with 1, 10, or 100 μ M, or 1, 10, or 100 nM Ang II for 24. 212 
Western blot analysis showed that stimulation with 10 uM Ang II significantly increased 213 
(P<0.05) (Fig. 1A and B) the expression of pro-Cat B (37 kDa) and active-Cat B (25 kDa) 214 
protein (Bien et al., 2004). A concentration of 100 μ M Ang II induced significant cell death 215 
which was confirmed by counting the number of cardiomyoblasts using a hemocytometer and 216 
comparing it to the non-treated set. This could likely be the reason for a decrease in Cat B 217 
protein expression at 100 μ M. 218 
 219 
3.2.Effect of EMD or CA-074Me on hypertrophy  220 
Previous reports have demonstrated that induction of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy due to 221 
various extracellular stimuli could be reduced by NHE1 inhibitors (Javadov et al., 2009; 222 
Journal Pre-proof
10 
Marano et al., 2004; Mohamed and Mraiche, 2015). In our study, 10 M of Ang II 223 
significantly enhanced the expression of ANP mRNA (P< 0.05) (Fig. 2A and B), total protein 224 
content (P< 0.05) (Fig. 2C) and cell surface area (P< 0.05) (Fig. 2D and E) in H9c2 225 
cardiomyoblasts. These effects were significantly regressed in the presence of EMD or CA-226 
074Me. 227 
 228 
3.3.Effect of EMD on Cat B protein and gene expression 229 
Protein and RNA samples extracted from EMD treated H9c2 cardiomyoblasts were subjected 230 
to Western blotting and RT PCR, respectively, to identify whether Cat B protein and mRNA 231 
expression are interrelated to the anti-hypertrophic property of EMD. Immunoblot and gene 232 
amplification analysis showed that Cat B protein and gene expression were significantly 233 
enhanced following treatment with 10 μ M Ang II (P<0.05), an effect that was significantly 234 
reduced in the presence of EMD (P<0.05) (Fig. 3A-D). Similarly, Cat B protein and gene 235 
expression were significantly reduced when pre-treated with CA-074Me (P<0.05) (Fig. 3A-236 
D).   237 
 238 
3.4.Effect of EMD or CA-074Me on lysosomal integrity  239 
The acidic environment within the lysosomes facilitates the localization of Cat B and 240 
functions to degrade and eliminate unwanted proteins (Cheng et al., 2012). In order to 241 
identify the effect of EMD or CA-074Me  on the localization and morphology of lysosomes, 242 
H9c2 cardiomyoblasts treated with 10 μ M Ang II in the presence of EMD or CA-074Me 243 
were stained with acidotropic probe LysoTracker Red. Stimulation of cardiomyoblasts with 244 
Ang II revealed an increased distribution and dispersion of the lysosomes when compared to 245 
the non-treated (Fig. 4A). Treatment with EMD or CA-074Me prevented the distribution of 246 
the lysosomes induced by Ang II (Fig. 4A).  247

3.5.Effect of EMD on Cat B protein expression in the extracellular environment 249 
Our findings suggest that treatment with EMD results in less dispersion and distribution of 250 
the lysosomes, which may be indicative of less extrusion of Cat B into the extracellular 251 
media.  Hence, we assessed the levels of Cat B in conditioned media of H9c2 252 
cardiomyoblasts treated with Ang II in the presence or absence of EMD or CA-074Me. 253 
Western blot analysis showed that Ang II treatment significantly increased Cat B protein 254 
(P<0.05) (Fig. 4B and C). The increase Cat B protein expression was significantly reduced in 255 
the presence of EMD (P<0.05) (Fig. 4B and C). Similarly, pre-treatment with CA-074Me 256 
significantly reduced the Cat B protein expression in the media (P<0.05) (Fig. 4B and C).  257 
 258 
3.6.Effect of EMD on MMP-9 gelatinolytic activity 259 
A recent study has shown that acidification of the extracellular space increases the secretion 260 
of MMP-9 and Cat B (Greco et al., 2014). It was also identified that Cat B is involved in the 261 
pH dependent activation of pro-MMP-9 (Giusti et al., 2008). Hence, we examined MMP-9 262 
activity in the conditioned media of H9c2 cardiomyoblasts stimulated with Ang II in the 263 
presence or absence of EMD or CA-074Me treatment. Gelatin zymography showed that 264 
MMP-9 gelatinolytic activity was significantly increased following stimulation with 10 μ M 265 
Ang II (P<0.05) (Fig. 4D and E), an effect that was significantly reduced upon stimulation 266 
with EMD (P<0.05) (Fig. 4D and E). Similarly, pre-treatment with CA-074Me significantly 267 
lowered MMP-9 activity (P<0.05) (Fig. 4D and E).  268 
 269 
3.7.Effect of EMD on the autophagic response  270 
Stress activated autophagy-lysosomal pathway is regulated by Cat B (activated by acidic pH) 271 
and NHE1 (Sun et al., 2013). LC3 II/I protein expression is generally used to assess 272

(Fig. 5A and B). 278

4. Discussion 280 
Activation and enhanced expression of NHE1 and proteases such as cathepsins have been 281 
proposed to contribute to CH (Cheng et al., 2012; Müller and Dhalla, 2012). NHE1 inhibitors 282 
have been shown to regress the hypertrophic response to various extracellular stimuli (Giusti 283 
et al., 2008; Kostoulas et al., 1999). Interestingly, Cat B in a breast cancer model was shown 284 
to be activated in response to a fall in the extracellular pH mediated by over active NHE1 285 
(Bourguignon et al., 2004). In addition, Cat B was demonstrated to directly interrelate with 286 
NHE1 and cause ECM degradation (Greco et al., 2014). Whether the anti-hypertrophic effect 287 
of NHE1 inhibition occurs due to the attenuation of Cat B remains unclear.  288 
 289 
4.1.Ang II induces the expression of Cat B in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts 290 
The role of Cat B in cardiac remodeling has been demonstrated in in vitro, in vivo and in 291 
human failing hearts. A previous in vitro study demonstrated that inhibition of Cat B reduced 292 
cardiac hypertrophy mediated through ASK1/JNK pathway (Wu et al., 2015). In vivo, 293 
pressure overload induced cardiac remodeling process was attenuated in the absence of Cat B 294 
(Wu et al., 2015). Moreover, the inhibition of Cat B with CA-074Me resulted in significant 295 
reduction of cardiomyocyte size, cardiac fibrosis and attenuated cardiac dysfunction. (Liu et 296 
Journal Pre-proof
13 
al., 2013). The expression of Cat B mRNA and protein were significantly higher in failing 297 
hearts compared with non-failing hearts (Ge et al., 2006).  298 
 299 
Our findings are in accordance with a previous study, which has demonstrated that Ang II 300 
induces Cat B expression/activity in the current study, both the pro and active-Cat B protein 301 
were significantly elevated in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts when treated with 10 μ M Ang II (Fig. 302 
1A and B). Similarly, Wu et al showed that Ang II increased Cat B protein expression in 303 
H9c2 cardiomyoblasts (Wu et al., 2015). 304 
 305 
Other cathepsin isoforms have also been implicated in cardiac remodeling and hypertrophy 306 
(Müller and Dhalla, 2012). A previous study showed that gene and protein levels of Cat S, B, 307 
and L were increased in cultured neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (Cheng et al., 2006). Similarly, 308 
Cat S has been shown to be elevated in the myocardium of rats linked with hypertension 309 
induced heart failure (Cheng et al., 2006). Cat S were also enhanced in hearts obtained from 310 
humans with heart failure (Cheng et al., 2006). Phenylephrine-induced CH was more 311 
prominent in the absence of Cat L, in vitro (Sun et al., 2013). Another study reported that MI 312 
induced by left coronary artery ligation in wild-type rats caused rapid Cat L activation in the 313 
myocardium and its deficiency resulted in reduced cardiac function and survival post-MI 314 
(Sun et al., 2011). Moreover, Cat S deficiency enhanced cardiac fibrosis stimulated by Ang II 315 
(Pan et al., 2012).  Although many forms of cathepsins have been proposed to contribute to 316 
CH, in our study we focused on Cat B, which is stimulated by an acidic pH (Bourguignon et 317 
al., 2004; Rozhin et al., 1994), a major stimulus of NHE1 activity. Whether the various forms 318 
of cathepsins are simultaneously upregulated in a model of hypertrophy or the interplay 319 
between the various forms of cathepsins remains unknown. 320

4.2.EMD reduces Cat B mediated hypertrophy  322 
In order to identify the role of Cat B in promoting hypertrophy, three hypertrophic markers 323 
namely, ANP mRNA, protein content and cell area were analyzed. Our results showed that 324 
10 μ M Ang II induced hypertrophy in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts (Fig. 2A-E). Inhibition of 325 
NHE1 or Cat B significantly decreased the hypertrophic effect (Fig. 2A-E). Pre-treatment 326 
with EMD significantly reduced Cat B protein and gene expression (Fig. 3A-D). Our results 327 
demonstrate for the first time that EMD reduced Cat B protein and mRNA expression in 328 
H9c2 cardiomyoblasts. Our findings reveal for the first time that the anti-hypertrophic effect 329 
of NHE1 inhibition may occur in part by reduced Cat B expression (Riaz, 2016).  330 
 331 
4.3.EMD or CA-074 reduces Cat B protein expression through MMP-9 activity 332 
The acidic environment within the lysosomes facilitates localization of Cat B where it 333 
becomes functional to degrade and eliminate defective proteins (Cheng et al., 2012). A 334 
previous report has confirmed that relocation of lysosomes to cell edge and secretion of Cat B 335 
is associated with an acidic extracellular pH. Interestingly, this effect was inhibited with 336 
various broad and specific NHE inhibitors (Steffan et al., 2009). Hence, we focused to 337 
analyze the intracellular localization of lysosomes using the acidotropic probe LysoTracker 338 
Red in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts stimulated with Ang II in the presence and absence of EMD. 339 
Treatment with EMD or CA-074Me resulted in less distribution and dispersion of the 340 
lysosomes (Fig. 4A), which may be indicative of less secretion of Cat B into the extracellular 341 
media. The dispersion of the lysosomes can result in the release of the Cat B proteases into 342 
the cytosol or into the extracellular compartment (Rozhin et al., 1994). Our study 343 
demonstrated for the first time that pro-Cat B protein levels were significantly increased in 344 
the extracellular compartment (Fig. 4B and C), an effect that was significantly reduced EMD 345 
or CA-074Me (Fig. 4B and C). It is to be noted that the pro-Cat B is enzymatically inactive, 346 
Journal Pre-proof
15 
however studies have shown that pro-Cat B can also be induced by interactions with 347 
matrices, for example, human prostate carcinoma cells with collagen I gels or human bone 348 
explants (Mundel and Reiser, 2010). It has been demonstrated that pro-Cat L could be 349 
activated by heparan sulphate (Ishidoh and Kominami, 1995).  350 
 351 
A previous study demonstrated that pro-Cat B retains some of its catalytic activity and as 352 
such, is capable of activating pro-MMP-9 (Giusti et al., 2008; Pungercar et al., 2009). 353 
Moreover, an in vivo study showed that CH does not occur in MMP-9
deficient mice after 354 
Ang II treatment, which suggests that MMP-9 has a crucial role in Ang II-induced cardiac 355 
hypertrophy (Weng et al., 2016). Therefore, we analyzed the MMP-9 gelatinolytic activity 356 
in the conditioned media. Gelatin zymography revealed that MMP-9 gelatinolytic activity 357 
was significantly increased in conditioned media from H9c2 cardiomyoblasts treated with 358 
Ang II (Fig. 4D and E). However, this effect was significantly reduced upon the inhibition of 359 
NHE1 or Cat B (Fig. 4D and E). In our study, MMP-9 was detected only at 92 kDa in the 360 
gelatin zymograms, which is in agreement with previous studies (Giusti et al., 2008; Solli et 361 
al., 2013). The band corresponding to 92 kDa is mostly described as the inactive MMP-9. 362 
However, certain protein substrates have been shown to cause conformational changes within 363 
the MMP-9 structure that result in the exposure of the active site without cleavage of the pro-364 
peptides (Fedarko et al., 2004; Freise et al., 2009).  365

4.4.EMD maintains the autosomal-lysosomal pathway   367 
Deregulation of various signaling pathways and cardiomyocyte autophagy lead to the 368 
development of pathological CH (Frey and Olson, 2003; Heineke and Molkentin, 2006). 369 
Foregoing research also proved that regulation of pH by NHE1 is crucial in regulating 370 
autophagy in order to eliminate defective proteins (Togashi et al., 2013). Whether the anti-371 
Journal Pre-proof
16 
hypertrophic effects of NHE1 inhibitors reduce Cat B in association with cardiomyocyte 372 
autophagy remains unclear.   373 
 374 
LC3 is a ubiquitously found soluble protein in mammalian tissues and cultured cells (Tanida 375 
et al., 2008). During autophagy, autophagosomes engulf cytoplasmic components, including 376 
cytosolic proteins and organelles. Alongside, a cytosolic form of LC3 (LC3-I) is conjugated 377 
to phosphatidylethanolamine to form LC3-II, which is transported to autophagosome 378 
membranes (Tanida et al., 2008). The final step of the autophagy–lysosomal pathway is the 379 
fusion of an autophagosome with a functioning lysosome. LC3-II stays on the membrane 380 
until it is degraded by the lysosome. Hence, the amount of LC3
‑II/LC3-I reflects autophagic 381 
activity, and detecting LC3 by immunoblotting has become a reliable and widely used marker 382 
for autophagic process (Levine and Kroemer, 2008; Mizushima and Yoshimori, 2007). It is to 383 
be noted that an imbalance of protein homeostasis by dysfunction of the autophagy-lyosomal 384 
pathway may lead to pathological hypertrophy (Sandri, 2013; Zhao et al., 2007).  385 
 386 
Our results showed that the ratio of LC3-II/LC3-I protein expression was significantly 387 
increased (Fig. 5A and B). This effect was significantly reduced upon treatment with EMD or 388 
CA-074Me (Fig. 5A and B). Our results showed that EMD or CA-074Me in H9c2 389 
cardiomyoblasts stimulated with Ang II reduced the expression of the ratio of LC3-II/LC3-I, 390 
which is indicative of impaired autophagy. 391

5. Conclusion 393 
The inhibition of NHE1 or Cat B reduced the hypertrophic response indicating an important 394 
role of NHE1 and Cat B in the hypertrophic pathway. Our study demonstrated for the first 395 
time that inhibition of NHE1 with EMD reduced Cat B protein and gene expression 396 
Journal Pre-proof
17 
suggesting that the anti-hypertrophic effect of EMD, an NHE1 inhibitor, is mediated by Cat 397 
B. Furthermore, inhibition of NHE1 and Cat B resulted in less dispersion of the lysosomes 398 
and also reduced the protein expression of the LC3II to LC3-I ratio. This suggests that the 399 
autophagy-lysosomal pathway plays a role in mediating the anti-hypertrophic. Dispersion of 400 
the lysosomes resulted in the secretion of Cat B into the extracellular space where it activated 401 
pro-MMP-9. These effects were reduced upon inhibition of NHE1 or Cat B (Fig 6) (Riaz, 402 
2016). The extent to which the conclusions of the present work may be applicable to native 403 
cardiomyoblasts and in vivo heart will require further studies using appropriate animal 404 
models. This shall confirm the results obtained in the present study.  405
